Weather: Standard summer fare, with a high of 89 and a slight chance of afternoon showers.

On this date in 1914, City Council approves funds for a new City Hall skyscraper, but the plans fall apart the next year due to a lack of support. (more)

After years of hype, a giant indoor waterpark and resort opens soon in Spotsylvania. Here’s what you need to know

Scheduled to open Nov. 12, Kalahari’s primary appeal is its sheer number of amenities, all of which are located within the hotel. The resort chain’s trademark is its giant indoor waterpark, and Virginia’s will have more than 50 waterslides, as well as an adults-only swim-up bar.

💰
The project cost $900 million to build out, and room rates, which include waterpark access, start at $415.

The Greater Richmond Convention Center’s general manager said he doesn't expect the resort to hurt the convention center’s business. Read more here.

Google’s Chesterfield data centers arrive amid shifting opinions on the facilities

The conversation around data centers has changed dramatically since Google began planning its three sites in the county.

📉
A VCU poll showed 81% of voters support making data centers pay additional fees to offset increased electricity demand.

A Chesterfield supervisor said Thursday that “the current board does not have an appetite for any more data centers.” Read more here.

City will receive early access to tax money, intends to use it to support RPS

Richmond Public Schools, finding itself in an $8.9 million budget hole, asked Mayor Danny Avula to give the district an unbudgeted $5.6 million tax payment from short-term lodging operators.

Avula agreed on Friday and set the process in motion, though final approval will have to come from City Council. Read more here.

The Richmonder 25: Firewood volunteer Steve Allen delivers warmth to Richmonders who need it

Allen is a longtime member in the all-volunteer Project W.A.R.M, which just marked its 50th year of delivering free firewood to the elderly, people with medical issues and others who might struggle to gather wood on their own. 

By taking wood into homes, Allen and other Project W.A.R.M. members often develop close relationships with those they help.

When the founder of the project died, Allen ensured the work would continue.

“He’s the glue that holds it together,” the founder's widow said. Read more here.
